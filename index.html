A short guide for friends and family

What is a bat mitzvah?

When a Jewish girl turns 13, she becomes a bat mitzvah, Hebrew for “daughter of the commandment.” It means she is now old enough to take responsibility for her own Jewish life. The ceremony is where she shows everyone she’s ready, and it takes years of work to get there. Later that night, everyone celebrates her hard work at a big party.

Chapter 1

Years of work for one morning

Most kids start Hebrew school around age 8, usually once or twice a week on top of regular school. In the last year or so, she meets one-on-one with a tutor, often every week, to prepare her part of the service.

Think of it like a big recital, except the music is in another language, thousands of years old, and the audience is everyone she knows.

Chapter 2

The hardest part: reading the scroll

The Torah is written by hand on a scroll, the same way it has been for thousands of years.

She has to know her portion well enough to read it and chant it to a traditional melody she memorizes, standing in front of the whole congregation.

She also writes a short speech about what her portion means to her.

The service is at the synagogue and usually lasts about an hour. Attendance is optional, but your support will mean a lot to her.

Chapter 3

What you’ll see on the day

  1. The service. Usually in a synagogue, often on a Saturday morning. She helps lead the prayers.
  2. The Torah reading. The scroll is carried out and opened, and she reads her portion. This is the moment she has worked toward.
  3. Her speech. She shares what she learned. Expect proud tears in the front rows.
  4. Candy! In many synagogues, guests toss soft candies toward her afterward to wish her a sweet life.
  5. The party. Later that night, it’s time to celebrate! Skip the dress shoes and wear your sneakers: the DJ will keep the high-energy music going for a top-notch teen dance party that lasts until nearly midnight. Dinner and dessert will be served. Watch for the hora, a circle dance where she’s lifted up in a chair.
Chapter 4

Why it matters so much

A bat mitzvah is one of the most important milestones in a Jewish person’s life, much like Confirmation in many Christian churches. It marks a rite of passage: from this day on, she is counted as an adult member of her community, responsible for her own choices and traditions.

For her family, it’s something even bigger: what was handed from grandparents to parents is now hers to carry forward.

Chapter 5

How to be a great guest

You don’t need to know anything or do anything special. Just being there means a lot.

Dress up

Dress as you would for a nice wedding (plus sneakers for the party).

Follow along

It’s fine to just sit quietly; most prayer books have English on the facing page. Stand when others stand.

Phones away

Many synagogues don’t allow phones or photos during Saturday services. Ask the family if you’re not sure.

Gifts

Money is common, often in multiples of $18, such as $36, $54, $72, and so on. The Hebrew letters for 18 spell chai, which means “life.”

Say “Mazel tov!”

It means congratulations. Say it like MAH-zel tov.

Most of all, tell her you’re proud of her.

She earned it.
